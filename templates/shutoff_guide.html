Emergency Homeowner First-Aid

Stop The Flood: 60-Second Main Water Shut-Off Guide

Every minute water sprays under 60 PSI, it pumps 8 to 12 gallons of water into your home. Follow these 4 steps immediately while your verified plumber is en route.

1

Find Your Main Water Shut-Off Location

The main line brings cold municipal or well water into your home. Look in one of these three primary spots:

Basement or Crawl Space

Look on the interior wall closest to the street, near ground level where the pipe pierces the foundation.

Utility Closet / Water Heater

In homes built on a concrete slab, check the utility room next to the water heater or near the washing machine.

Outdoor Curb Water Meter Box

Look in the front yard near the street curb for a cast-iron or concrete ground box labeled "WATER".

2

Identify Your Valve Type & Turn It Off

Type A: Modern Homes Easiest

Ball Valve (Lever Handle)

A straight lever handle. When open, it runs parallel to the pipe.

Action: Turn 90 Degrees Perpendicular

Turn lever a quarter-turn until it forms a cross (90-degree right angle) with the pipe. Water is now shut off.

Type B: Older Homes Caution: Do Not Force

Gate Valve (Round Wheel)

A round wheel handle similar to an outdoor garden spigot.

Action: Turn Clockwise (Righty-Tighty)

Turn clockwise multiple rotations until snug. If valve is stiff or corroded, do NOT wrench with force (can snap stem); spray WD-40 or wait for plumber curb key.

3

Relieve Trapped Line Pressure

Even with the main valve closed, several gallons of water remain trapped under static pressure inside upper pipes.

Open Lowest Faucet: Turn on the basement utility sink or outdoor garden spigot completely.
Open Highest Faucet: Turn on a top-floor bathroom sink to break the vacuum seal and drain the pipes safely into drains instead of your floors.

Water Shut Off Completed?

Return to the emergency dispatch screen with confirmed status to speed up the repair process.
